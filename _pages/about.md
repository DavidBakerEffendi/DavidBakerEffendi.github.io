I build open-source, multi-language code intelligence for coding agents at [Brokk](https://brokk.ai). [Bifrost](https://github.com/BrokkAi/bifrost) gives agents and developer tools a structural view of unbuilt or partially broken repositories, including mixed-language workspaces.

The analysis stack is available as a Rust crate, MCP server, LSP, and CLI. Its S-expression query language supports portable structural queries, with semantic layers for relationships such as usages, imports, and type hierarchies built on demand. My work sits at the intersection of static analysis, program verification, and AI-assisted software engineering.

## Background

From 2022 to June 2025, I was employed at [Whirly Labs](https://whirlylabs.com) to lead the development of tailored static analysis solutions for clients, serving as the primary interface between Whirly Labs and its partners. My role included overseeing the creation and maintenance of tools while providing training materials to enable client teams to contribute effectively to these codebases.

I graduated with my Ph.D. in [Computer Science at Stellenbosch University](https://www.sun.ac.za/english/pgstudies/Pages/Science/Computer-Science.aspx) in March 2025. My PhD thesis, titled "An Approach to Modern Static Analysis with Property Graphs", addresses practical limitations in static analysis of large-scale programs through a language-agnostic approach. This research leverages a combination of property graphs on database backends, parallel processing, and, in some cases, machine learning to overcome performance bottlenecks and enhance analysis maintainability for analyzing multiple languages. 

I maintain a connection to the university through co-supervising postgraduate students on research topics related to static program analysis.
